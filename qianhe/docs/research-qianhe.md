# Autopsy: Qianhe (千和投) — Failed Startup Case Study

*Research compiled via the startup-autopsy workflow from loot-drop.io + primary-source cross-verification. Date: 2026-08-01.*

## 1. The Company

Chinese **peer-to-peer (P2P) lending platform** connecting individual lenders with underbanked SMEs.

- **Sector:** Financials / Fintech
- **Country:** China
- **Years active:** 2014–2019 (loot-drop claim — founding year unverified; platform confirmed active by March 2015)
- **Total cash burned:** $100.0M (loot-drop claim — NOT confirmed by primary sources)
- **Product type:** Financial & Fintech

## 2. Cause of Death

1. **Regulatory crackdown** — China's 2019–2020 government purge of the P2P industry (from ~6,000 platforms at peak to near zero).
2. **Regulatory arbitrage, not a moat** — the model depended on operating in a licensing gray zone with immature risk management.
3. **Unsustainable unit economics** — required continuous growth to work; systemic fraud and defaults compounded.

## 3. Market Before & Today

- **Then (2014–2017):** China's underbanked SME sector, risk-averse banks, mobile internet adoption. P2P market reached ~$200B+ transactions at 2017 peak, 3,383+ platforms active (Jan 2016).
- **Now:** Consumer-facing P2P lending is effectively dead post-2019. The SME financing gap (~$2.5T/yr) remains, but credit is channeled through licensed banks, microlenders, and fintech infrastructure. The winner path is **embedded/B2B lending**, not direct platforms.

## 4. Lessons

1. **Regulatory arbitrage is not a moat — it's a time bomb.** In financial services, if your edge is a loophole, a government can close it overnight.
2. **Unvalidated underwriting at scale = systemic defaults.** Growth-based models collapse when the funding treadmill stops.
3. **Separate capital supply from technology** — partner with licensed lenders; don't lend directly without a license.
4. **Sell to platforms, not consumers** — sidestepping regulatory risk while capturing the same market demand.

## 5. Rebuild Opportunity

Loot-drop's proposed "ChainCredit": a **B2B embedded-lending infrastructure** platform providing AI-driven underwriting, loan origination, and servicing APIs to licensed banks/microlenders and vertical SaaS platforms (e-commerce, logistics, agriculture). Revenue: per-API-call fees + risk-sharing on interest spread + SaaS subscriptions. My read: directionally sound and mirrors how the Chinese fintech market actually consolidated post-2019.
